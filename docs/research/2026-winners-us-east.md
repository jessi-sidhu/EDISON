# What won at US East Coast / Northeast projects in 2026

Research notes for the Edison team. Compiled 2026-09-26. Only events held in 2026 with winners already announced are covered.

## How this was gathered (and what was blocked)

- **Devpost events.** For each event I pulled the challenge page (prize list and judging criteria) and the project gallery. Devpost lists winners first, so I followed every "Winner" entry and scraped **each winning project's own Devpost page**: tagline, full write-up, "Built with" tags, "Try it out" links and the "Winner" labels. Search snippets were used only to find events.
- **HackMIT 2026 does not use Devpost.** It uses its own platform, Plume (`plume.hackmit.org`). The gallery page is a JavaScript app, but its public JSON endpoint (`/api/v3/projects/gallery?project_id=hack-2026&winners_only=true`) returns every winner with its full write-up. The sponsor-challenge and prize text came from the two Google Docs that `dayof.hackmit.org` embeds.
- **Blocked or failed:**
  - **Devpost (AWS WAF):** two project pages returned `HTTP 202` with `x-amzn-waf-action: challenge` to scripted requests. They were `devpost.com/software/search-and-rescue-2pwlkf` (the HooHacks 1st-place winner) and `devpost.com/software/search-and-rescue-bot` (a MakeMIT winner). I read both in a real Chrome tab instead, so no data is missing.
  - **HackNYU:** `hacknyu.org` refused connections (`ECONNREFUSED 167.71.252.160:443`) from both curl and the fetch tool. Devpost has no HackNYU 2026 event, so there were no HackNYU winners to verify.
  - **Web search budget:** the session ran out of web searches partway through. Event discovery after that used Devpost's public project-search API (`devpost.com/api/projects?search=...`) instead.
- **Judges' comments.** Devpost and Plume publish no judge feedback for any of these events. Every "why it won" note below is my **inference** from the published judging criteria, the sponsor rubric and the project write-up. Treat those notes as unverified.
- **"AI use (auto-tagged)" and "HW?" columns.** These are keyword heuristics over each project's write-up and tags. They are handy for skimming but can be wrong in both directions. The hand-written "Headline winners" tables were checked by reading the project pages.

## 1. HackMIT 2026 — Sep 19–20, 2026 (MIT)

**Links:** [hackmit.org](https://hackmit.org) · [winners gallery on Plume](https://plume.hackmit.org/gallery?project_id=hack-2026) · [dayof.hackmit.org](https://dayof.hackmit.org). 299 projects were submitted and 50 took at least one prize.

**Tracks:** Beginner, General, Healthcare, Education, Entertainment, Sustainability, plus 1st/2nd/3rd Overall. The overall tiers had prize budgets of about $8k, $4k and $2k per team; track 1st/2nd were about $1.5k and $1k per team.

**Sponsor challenges (23):** Long Lake "Convince a Non-Believer" (an AI experience a skeptic would love) · ASUS / Zenni Claw · **Maximor "Office of the CFO Agent"** ($4k/$2k/$1k; wants multi-step tool use, learning from previous runs, handling of exceptions, measurable improvement and human escalation — "a one-shot chatbot … is not enough") · Voloridge "Signal in the Noise" (public datasets, $5k) · Arduino "Touch Grass" (Arduino UNO Q, real-world sensing) · Visa "Reimagine Shopping" (GenAI commerce, $5k) · Cognition "Best Use of Devin" ($5k) · Warp "Best Developer Tool" · Espressif (AIoT, judged virtually) · Dimensional (agentic robotics on dimOS; prize is a Unitree Go2) · **OpenAI "The Fifth Teammate"** (OpenAI API plus Codex as a dev teammate; the demo must show one concrete way Codex helped) · Elastic "Find the Signal" · The Token Company (LLM cost saving) · **ElevenLabs** (rubric: "Agentic Depth … autonomous agents that handle complex logic and real-time dialogue", low latency, novelty) · **Meta** "Bringing People Closer Together with AI" (Muse Spark API; top 3 go to a round-2 project at Menlo Park) · GiveCampus (fundraising data) · Ramp "Save Time. Save Money." · SpaceXAI "Make it Legendary" (must use Cursor plus the Grok Imagine or Voice API) · Dropbox "Turn Digital Chaos Into Something Useful" · **Deepgram** "Build Something Worth Talking To" · Arrowstreet (greenwashing text analysis) · Hackster/Nordic (hardware) · **Regeneron** (clinical trials and biostatistics; judged on utility, readiness, and design and packaging, including a demo video and a 10–12 slide deck).

**Agent-heavy sponsor winners worth studying:**
- **Maximor "Office of the CFO Agent" (5 places, all agent systems).** 1st [VistaAI](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/cslzr-pathl-qrbxv-xahcq) (an AI operating layer for private-equity roll-ups: ingests messy CSV/XLSX, proposes schema mappings, human review queue) · 2nd [CFO-Sabbatical](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/zwjij-edzdv-vmitm-apwhq) (15 Grok agents over Gmail and Stripe invoices) · 3rd [Sherlock](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/cxwit-mchwb-zrdld-pegqj) (a 22-role LangGraph "finance org" with checkpointed human-in-the-loop) · 4th [Money Maxer](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/nlhtb-gqmiu-yvcyw-pibeg) (Claude Agent SDK; tiered models under budgets; deterministic checks on a single write path) · 5th [TrueUp](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/bktpj-dafuf-nalsl-ctlel) ("LLM agents reason over ambiguous documents, deterministic code owns dollar calculations").
- **OpenAI 2nd + Regeneron Grand Prize: [Elute](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/afzbr-ptgza-tcorl-mrobb).** An agentic orchestrator that stress-tests drug–disease hypotheses. It decomposes the question into scientific subtasks and routes them to real tools (Harvard ToolUniverse, Open Targets and others), then surfaces the weakest links.
- **Cognition "Best Use of Devin": [Kingdom](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/joohi-tssln-wtyad-hmqnd).** Every hypothesis becomes a "Mission" that runs its own Devin session. Missions can branch and be killed, and they feed a live research thread.
- **ElevenLabs winner: [RSpace](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/gmbqz-ythjg-mqrfp-aklbo).** A one-button voice companion for older adults that remembers facts across conversations and introduces them to real people with shared interests. Its write-up explains "why AI is load-bearing".
- **Deepgram winner: [Braillie](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/qhrlf-bsrzd-xinnt-pvcbe).** A phone camera tracks a fingertip on a printed braille sheet and coaches the learner by voice. Fully voice-operated for blind users.
- **Dropbox 1st: [Recall](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/cocds-fxbwj-czmjd-uzpbs).** A voice companion that proactively calls people with dementia for active-recall sessions, grounded in a family-photo knowledge graph.
- **Token Company (LLM cost): [Pilot](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/miaaf-vqolp-etbqr-beghb).** An agent explores a website once and compiles a reusable, versioned extraction function, so later agents don't pay for browser automation again.
- **Meta 1st + Espressif: [Klick](https://plume.hackmit.org/project/rwqwq-hqecj-bfxns-lyqyc).** Everyone gets an AI twin; when two people walk past each other (BLE), their twins chat, and if there's a reason to meet, both lanyard badges light the same colour.

## Patterns

These are drawn from about 415 winning entries across the 2026 events above. HackMIT, HackPrinceton, Bitcamp, YHack, HopHacks, HooHacks and DevFest carry the most weight. By the auto-tag: about 2/3 of winners name a specific LLM, about 1/3 describe agents, about 1/3 include hardware, and about 87% submitted a demo video.

1. **Grand prizes went to "AI + something physical or measurable", not to chat apps.** HackMIT's podium was a pill-dissolution lab, a from-scratch GPU and a robotic caregiver chair. YHack's top 3 were an irrigation turret, a surgical simulator and a gait wearable. Hack@Brown's winner was a sorting bin. HopHacks' three co-winners were a vaccine cold-chain sensor, an object finder for blind users and drone tracking. MakeMIT's grand prizes had no LLM at all. **Pure-software grand prizes did exist** (Asclepius at HackPrinceton, Seamless at DevFest, Aether and Furrow at Bitcamp, Search and Rescue and Agent Gauntlet at HooHacks), and every one of them had a very specific user and workflow.
2. **When agents won, they were one of two kinds.** Some were **agents embedded where the user already works**: Burrow's in-browser tutor, Second Self's parallel macOS session, Furrow over SMS/iMessage, LEGR/Shomi/Wavelength in iMessage via Photon, Sprynt joining incident calls, CareLoop and Recall placing phone calls. The others were **multi-agent pipelines over real tools and data**: Elute (ToolUniverse), the Maximor CFO agents, Rent-Swarm, Tabula, Kingdom (one Devin session per hypothesis). Generic "chat with X" wrappers basically never took a top prize.
3. **Judges and sponsors rewarded guardrails and verification inside agent systems.** Winning write-ups repeatedly say "deterministic code owns the numbers" (TrueUp, Money Maxer), "a regex safety tier before the LLM" (CareLoop), "every patch verified by re-running the exploit" (arena), "the model never sees the product page, to block prompt injection" (Argus), "architecturally incapable of leaking answers" (Burrow) and "human approval before sending" (DonoRex, Sherlock). Maximor's rubric required learning from runs, measurable improvement and human escalation. The Claude projects scored "beyond a chatbot wrapper" and ethical alignment.
4. **Voice is everywhere, and it wins when it is the interface a specific user needs.** Examples: Braillie (blind learners), RSpace (older adults, one button), Lil-Vro (kids, no screen), Recall (dementia), Furrow (farmers' voice notes). ElevenLabs and/or Deepgram ran a prize at almost every event. The ElevenLabs HackMIT rubric asked for agentic depth and low latency, not just TTS.
5. **The problem framing was hyper-specific and backed by numbers.** Winners open with a named user and a statistic: "1 in 10 medical products in LMICs substandard" (Peel), "85% of COPD patients misuse inhalers" (Cherry Canary), "nurses hand off patients from memory after 12-hour shifts" (Relay), "31K Opus calls that should've been Haiku — $2,840/mo" (LEGR). Healthcare, accessibility (blind, deaf, dementia, tremor) and elder care together make up the single biggest cluster of winners.
6. **Stacking sponsor prizes is a real strategy.** CareChair took four prizes (OpenAI, ASUS, Dimensional, 3rd Overall). Axis took twelve at LIVE AI. Asclepius and GreenChain took three each. Winners deliberately use several sponsor tools (Gemini, ElevenLabs, Photon, Dedalus, K2 Think, Snowflake, Solana, Auth0) where each one does real work. MLH "Best Use of X" prizes were awarded at almost every Devpost event.
7. **Demo style: live, physical, or instantly playable.** Winners judges could try on the spot included ClashMIT (open a link and shout spells), SUPERMECHAFIGHTER (stomp on a pad), Hop-It (draw on paper, then play), Someone Save Us (text a number) and DoortectiveAI (People's Choice). About 87% of winners had a demo video, and the strong ones show a working end-to-end loop rather than mockups. Regeneron also asked for a slide deck and an MIT-licensed repo.
8. **Hardware vs software.** About 1/3 of winners include hardware by the auto-tag (far more at MakeMIT and Reality Hack). Hardware is over-represented among **overall** winners. *Unverified*: I could not get submission-level hardware counts to compare against. The cheap kit that won most often: ESP32, Arduino UNO Q, Raspberry Pi, RealSense, webcams with MediaPipe/YOLO, and smart glasses (Meta Ray-Ban, RayNeo, Snap Spectacles).

**Implication for an AI-agent team (my read, not verified):** pick one narrow user with a costly, repetitive task. Put the agent where that user already is (SMS, phone call, browser, glasses). Give it real tools or data and deterministic guardrails. Show one end-to-end live action in the demo, and if you can, add a cheap physical sensor or actuator that makes the result tangible.
